## Review (Claude, 2026-11-04, against the brief as revised in vynr-web #36)

**Accepted as the anatomical sketch, with four corrections to carry into the
vector redraw.** It does not advance past the sketch gate on its own: the
corrections below are the gate's output.

What is right:

- Five-digit grip that reads: thumb pad on the near side of the stem under
  the bowl, index curled behind the stem with its tip hidden, middle, ring
  and little fingers wrapped round with their tips returning toward the
  viewer. The dark wedge inside the fist is a good linocut device; keep it.
- The stem is continuous: it enters under the thumb and leaves under the
  little finger on one axis.
- The bowl is the app icon's bowl, swirl up the right side, one cream sliver
  as the highlight. That is already the brief's exposed-garment highlight.
- The wider foot, about 80% of bowl width, reads as a foot rather than a pin.

Corrections:

1. **Foot tangent to the wrist.** The foot's left tip lands on the wrist
   outline. The foot is in front of the wrist, so overlap the wrist clearly
   and interrupt the wrist line behind it, by about a tenth of the foot's
   width. (A clear gap is the alternative; it reads as floating.)
2. **Foot and rim disagree about eye level.** The rim is almost edge-on, the
   foot an open ellipse seen from above. Flatten the foot ellipse to about
   three fifths of its current height at the same width. The icon's rim is
   near edge-on too, so flatten the foot rather than open the rim.
3. **Stem exit.** A small flare under the little finger reads as a knop.
   One stem, one width, to the foot.
4. **Scale is poster scale; keep it.** The glass is about 1.5× life against
   this hand. That is what gives the stem room for three fingers and keeps
   the bowl the hero. The illustrator must not "correct" it; recorded as an
   invariant in the brief.

Notes, not corrections: this is an outline drawing, not yet a linocut; the
cuts, the hatching on the hand and the wrist bone come at the vector stage per
the brief. The three square fingernails are a little cartoon; each becomes one
nick in the cut version.
